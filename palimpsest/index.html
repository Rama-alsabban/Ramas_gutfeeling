Palimpsest

A small language model lives on this page. It learned to write by counting which word follows which in about eighty ordinary sentences. Teach it a secret, delete the secret, and then try to make it forget. Big models learn much richer patterns, but they have the same problem.

    Conversation

    Start a sentence and the model finishes it, one predicted word at a time.

    Teach it a secret

    Something small and true. It stays in this browser tab, isn't sent anywhere and is gone when you close the page. The model will read it six times, the way large models end up memorising text they see repeatedly.

    Make it forget

    Delete the message first. Then try the three ways a model can be made to forget.

    Subtract the secret

    Remove exactly the counts your secret added. This is the clean way, and it needs the secret's exact text.

    Unavailable: you deleted the text it needs.

    The tracer ink on this page is for you. The model's tools can't see it, and real models don't have it.

    Prune rare words

    Cut the least-used words first, without knowing which ones were yours. Your secret was repeated, so it doesn't look rare.

    Drag it, then continue a sentence to hear the damage.

    Retrain from scratch

    Throw the model away and rebuild it from its original reading, which never included your secret.

    Here it takes a second. For a large model it means repeating the whole training run, which is rarely done for one request.

    The cost of forgetting

    Each dot is one pruning level. Lower means less of the secret remains. Further right means more of everything else survives.

    Pruning slides down and to the left together. The corner you want, where the secret is gone and nothing else is lost, is only reached by retraining, and that produces a new model.
